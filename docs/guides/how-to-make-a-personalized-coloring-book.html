How to make a personalized coloring book

A coloring book made from your own photos is a gift that nobody else can buy: the family dog, grandma's house, the trip to the lake. Here is how to plan one, print it and put it together, with or without an app.

Start with the reader, not the photos

Before you pick pictures, think about who will color the book. That decides almost everything else.

  • Toddlers and preschoolers want big shapes and thick lines. Fewer, simpler pages work best, and a favorite pet or toy beats a complicated scene.
  • School-age children can handle more detail. Mix easy pages with a few that take longer.
  • Older kids and adults often enjoy fine, detailed lines and a place or a building they know.

It also helps to think about the moment. A book for a long car ride, a birthday party favor or a grandparent's visit can each have a different feel.

Choose and order the pages

Ten to twenty pages is plenty for most books. Fewer pages make a lovely small gift, and many more tend to get abandoned half way through.

  • Pick photos that turn into good line art: one clear subject, a simple background, good light.
  • Mix subjects: a portrait, an animal, a place, a day out. Repeating the same kind of picture gets dull.
  • Put an easy, satisfying page first, so the first page gets finished and the book gets picked up again.
  • Keep the very detailed pages for the middle or the end.
  • Think of one page for the cover, usually the best photo.

Cover, name and title page

What makes a coloring book feel personal is the cover. A title ("Ella's Summer Book") and the reader's name on the front turn a stack of pages into a book. A "This book belongs to" page near the front adds a spot for the owner to sign. Page numbers help when pages come loose and need putting back in order.

Print and bind it

  • Print one-sided. Markers and crayons press through, and a drawing on the back of a page ruins the page behind it.
  • Use paper that suits the pens. Plain paper is fine for crayons and pencils. For markers, use heavier paper.
  • Print at actual size on Letter or A4, whichever your printer uses.
  • Bind it simply. A staple or two down the left edge works. A hole punch with ribbon or a binder clip is easy to take apart. A print shop can add a card cover and spiral binding if you want it to last.
  • Print a spare. A second copy of the cover and favorite pages is handy when the first one gets colored.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing too many pages and too many busy photos, so the book never gets finished.
  • Printing double-sided with markers.
  • Forgetting the paper size, then finding the cover is cut off.
  • Leaving out the name. It is a small detail that matters a lot to the child holding the book.

Doing it with Linemade

In Linemade you make pages from your photos first (see the guide on turning a photo into a coloring page), then collect them into a book.

  1. Open the Books tab and tap New Book.
  2. Give the book a title and the reader's name. They go on the cover.
  3. Tap Choose Pages and pick your pages. Drag to reorder them, and touch and hold a page to put it on the cover.
  4. Choose A4 or Letter paper, and choose whether to include a "This book belongs to" page. Page numbers are added for you.
  5. Open the book and tap Preview & Print to see the whole PDF.
  6. Tap Print to send it to an AirPrint printer, or Share PDF to choose Save to Files, send it by AirDrop or Mail, or open it in another app to print later.
