How to turn a photo into a coloring page

A coloring page is a photo with everything removed except the outlines. Done well, you get clean lines to print and color for a rainy afternoon, a party table or a gift. Here is what makes a photo work, the ways to do it, and the quickest route on an iPhone.

What a good coloring page needs

Most of what you see in a photo is not useful for coloring: the colors, the soft shading, the texture of fur or fabric. What you want is the edges, the places where one shape stops and another starts.

A page is easy to color when it has a few clear shapes, lines that join up so a crayon has somewhere to stop, and plenty of empty space. A page is frustrating when it has hundreds of tiny areas, broken lines or dark patches where the photo had a shadow.

Pick a photo that will work

The photo decides most of the result. A good one has:

  • One clear subject. A dog, a person, a house, a boat. Group shots work, but each extra subject adds lines.
  • A plain or simple background. Sky, a wall or a blurred background all turn into calm empty space. Grass, leaves, brick and cluttered rooms turn into a storm of lines.
  • Even, soft light. Harsh sun creates deep shadows, and shadows can end up as black blotches or odd shapes. Shade or an overcast day is kinder.
  • A subject that fills the frame. A small figure in a big scene gets very few lines of its own. Move closer or crop.
  • Sharp focus. A blurry photo has soft edges, and soft edges become weak, broken lines.

Pets and portraits are a great start, especially when the animal or person looks at the camera against a plain background.

Three ways to make one

1. Trace it by hand

Print the photo, tape it to a window or a light box, lay thin paper over it and trace the outlines with a black pen. You choose every line, so the result can be lovely, but it takes a while and needs a steady hand.

2. Use an edge filter in a photo editor

Desktop editors such as GIMP and Photoshop have edge-detection filters. They find the places where brightness changes sharply and turn them into lines. The catch is that a general filter finds every bit of texture too, so you usually need to adjust thresholds and clean up by hand.

3. Use an app made for it

A coloring-page app does the edge finding, cleaning and printing in one place. That is the point of Linemade, described below. Whichever route you take, the photo advice above applies.

Print it so it colors well

  • Print at 100% (actual size). Scaling to fit can shrink the margins and thin out the lines.
  • Use black only. If your printer has a draft or ink-saver mode, switch it off for line art, or the lines come out pale.
  • Choose the paper for the pens. Plain printer paper is fine for crayons and colored pencils. Markers bleed through it, so use heavier paper or slip a spare sheet underneath.
  • Check Letter versus A4. The two sizes are close but not identical. Pick the one your printer holds so nothing gets cut off.
  • Test one page first. It is much cheaper than printing ten pages and finding the lines are too faint.

Common mistakes

  • Using a photo with a busy background and wondering why the page looks scribbled.
  • Making the lines too thin for small children. Thick, simple lines are easier to stay inside.
  • Choosing a very dark or very bright photo. Both lose detail in the shadows or highlights.
  • Using a tiny, low-resolution image. Small photos give rough lines when printed large.
  • Expecting every hair or leaf to survive. Good coloring pages leave things out on purpose.

Doing it with Linemade

Linemade turns a photo into line art on your iPhone in a few seconds. The photo is processed on the device and is not uploaded.

  1. Open the Pages tab and tap Choose a Photo. You can also take a photo with the camera, import one from Files, or start with one of the included example photos.
  2. Linemade draws your photo as line art. Drag the handle across the picture to compare it with the original.
  3. Pick a style: Bold for thick, simple outlines, Classic for an everyday page, or Fine for more, thinner lines.
  4. Fine-tune with the Detail slider (fewer or more lines) and the Lines slider (thin or thick).
  5. If the background is busy, turn on Remove background to keep just the pet or person. This switch only appears on devices that support Apple's subject lifting, and only when Linemade finds a clear subject.
  6. Tap Save. The page goes into your library.
  7. Open the page and tap Print to send it to an AirPrint printer, or Share to make a PDF.

Prefer to color on the phone? Open the page and tap Color It In. With Fill, tap an area and the color stays inside the lines. There are also Marker, Crayon and Eraser brushes, and Undo.

Making pages is unlimited on the free plan. You get 3 prints or PDF exports a week, with a small "Made with Linemade" footer on the page. Linemade Pro removes the weekly limit and lets you turn the footer off. If your page looks too busy, the help page has a short checklist.
