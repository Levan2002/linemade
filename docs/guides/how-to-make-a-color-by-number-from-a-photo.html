How to make a color by number from your own photo

Most color-by-number books come with someone else's pictures. You can make your own from a photo of a pet, a house or a holiday. This guide explains how a page like that is built, what to watch out for, and how to make one on an iPhone.

How a color-by-number page works

A color-by-number page has three parts:

  1. Areas. The picture is divided into regions, each bounded by a line.
  2. Numbers. Each region carries a number.
  3. A key. A list that says which color goes with each number.

The fun is in the finishing. When every area is filled in, the picture takes the shape of the original photo, in simplified colors.

What you need to build one

Making one by hand means three jobs: reduce the photo to a small set of colors, split it into areas, and place a number in each area. Photo editors can do the first step with a posterize filter, which merges similar colors into a few flat ones. Outlining areas and numbering them is the slow part, and it is easy to end up with specks of one color scattered across the page.

That is why it is worth using a tool that does the grouping for you, and why the choice of photo matters so much.

How many colors should you use?

The number of colors sets how hard the page is.

  • Around 4 to 8 colors suits young children or a quick page. Areas are larger and the key is short.
  • Around 10 to 16 colors suits school-age children and relaxed adults.
  • 20 or more keeps more of the photo's subtle shading, and suits patient colorers who enjoy a long project.

More colors also means smaller areas and a longer key, and some neighboring colors look alike on paper. Start low, look at the result, and increase if the picture looks too flat.

Choose the photo carefully

  • Bold, distinct colors turn into clear areas. A muted, gray photo gives drab, similar colors that are hard to tell apart.
  • Simple shapes make larger areas that are easier to fill in.
  • A plain background avoids a mosaic of tiny regions. Sky, water or a wall behind the subject work well.
  • Good light keeps shadows from becoming extra colors you did not want.

Printing tips

Print the page and the color key together, at actual size, so the numbers stay legible. Keep the printed key beside the page while you color. Colored pencils or fine markers give the most control inside small areas. If you want to match the original photo, pick colors from the key that look closest.

Doing it with Linemade

Color-by-number pages are part of Linemade Pro. Linemade reads the colors in your photo, numbers every area and builds the key for you.

  1. Open the Pages tab and tap Choose a Photo.
  2. While you are making the page, turn on Color by number. If you are not on Linemade Pro, the paywall appears here.
  3. Choose how many colors to use.
  4. Linemade numbers every area and adds a color key so you know which color goes where.
  5. Adjust the Detail slider or the style if the page has too many small areas, then tap Save.
  6. Open the page and tap Print. The key comes with the page.

If your page has lots of tiny areas, try fewer colors, the Bold style, or a photo with a plainer background. The help page has a short section on color by number.
